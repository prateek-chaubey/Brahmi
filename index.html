Brahmi, the mother script

Brahmi is among the oldest writing systems of the Indian subcontinent, in documented use from roughly the 7th century BCE through the 5th century CE. It rose to lasting fame as the script of Emperor Ashoka's 3rd-century BCE stone edicts, carved onto pillars and rock faces across his empire to spread messages of dharma. Nearly every major script used across South and Southeast Asia today — Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, Kannada, Thai, Tibetan, and many more — traces its lineage back to Brahmi.

How this tool works

Each Devanagari letter, vowel sign, numeral and punctuation mark is mapped to its corresponding character in the Unicode Brahmi block (U+11000–U+1107F), following the official Unicode character tables. Conversion happens character by character in your browser — nothing is sent to a server.

Fonts & references

Brahmi glyphs render using Google's Noto Sans Brahmi font. Devanagari is set in Noto Serif Devanagari. For a deeper dive, see the Unicode Brahmi code chart and the Brahmi script entry on Wikipedia.
